Reading a Label in Ten Seconds

  1. Skip the front of the pack. “Natural”, “wholesome” and “heart-healthy” are marketing. The facts are on the back.
  2. Read the ingredient list. Ingredients are listed by weight, most first. The first three tell you what you are mostly eating.
  3. Count and picture. If the list is long, or you can’t picture an ingredient growing or sitting in a kitchen, think twice.

Ingredients to Watch For

If any of these is on the label, put it back, or at least think twice.

  • Lecithin
  • Xanthan gum, and other gums
  • Shortening, margarine
  • “Partially hydrogenated” anything
  • Canola oil
  • Colourings
  • Natural flavorings
  • Annatto
  • Olive oil without a harvest date. Look for single origin.

The Pocket Guide

Eating Out

What to watch for when someone else is cooking.

Watch Out

Ingredients

  • Lecithin
  • Xanthan gum, other gums
  • Shortening, margarine
  • “Partially hydrogenated”
  • Canola oil
  • Colourings
  • Natural flavorings
  • Annatto
  • EVOO should be single origin, with a harvest date

Preparation

  • Fried food (including chips and fries)
  • Mayo, dressings on top
  • Whipped “butter”

Look and Taste

  • Oils with a chemical taste
  • Shiny meat (probably brushed with oil)
  • “Buttery” looking bread

Yes

  • Pickles, kraut, kimchi
  • Breads, crackers (dry)
  • Baked, grilled or steamed
  • Thin-crust pizza
  • Spaghetti, pho, ramen
  • Nigiri, maki, rolls (no mayo)
  • Oysters, mussels, shrimp

Yes, If…

  • Condiments (if you can check the ingredients)
  • Food fried in duck fat, tallow, coconut oil or pork fat (lard)
  • Oils that are extra virgin, cold pressed or expeller pressed
  • Take a bite, wait 7 minutes, see how you feel
