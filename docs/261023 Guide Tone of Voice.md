# Johnny B. Quacker: tone of voice

Drawn from Johnny's own messages and recipe sheets. Use it for anything written on the site, whether it's Johnny's text, our edits, or new copy.

## In one line

**Sharing, not crusading.** A plain-spoken guy telling you what worked for him, the hard way, so you can take it or leave it.

## What Johnny believes

These shape the content as much as the voice.

1. **The answer is usually right under your nose.** You just have to look at it the right way. Most of this is obvious in hindsight.
2. **There is no conspiracy.** A corporation's only ethic is to maximise profit and minimise loss. That explains most of the food supply without anyone being a villain.
3. **Legal predation isn't a crime.** Much of what hurts us is perfectly lawful.
4. **It's not the reader's fault.** We're only victims because our great-grandparents were busy with a century of world wars and nuclear terror.
5. **The agencies aren't for your health.** The FDA exists to prevent immediate mass chaos. The USDA exists to sell product. Health is the cover story.
6. **Health is the whole point.** That means health of body, mind and money, and the ability to live freely with the least risk. *Feel better, spend less, risk less.*
7. **Stopping the damage isn't the same as healing.** Decades of damage aren't undone in a few months. In Johnny's words, it's not "well, I stopped punching you in the face last night, so what's the problem this morning?" Recovery takes time. Even real food can be hard for a while.

## How he sounds

| Trait | Looks like |
|---|---|
| **Plain and direct** | Short sentences. "Stir and leave it alone." "Use it or leave it." |
| **Wry, not angry** | Dry humour carries the point: "Or, long fingernails?" for tongs, or "Dear Paella Policia". He's never shrill. |
| **Playful with words** | Puns and spellings like "Wee Thu Peeps", "the whey I put it", "Food®". Use one now and then in asides and intros, never in instructions. |
| **Capitals for the one word that matters** | BURNING. ALMOST. WHISPERING. One word per sentence, at most. |
| **Everyday comparisons** | Rice that "crinkles like tissue paper" and "crackles like note paper". Sensory cues beat numbers when cooking. |
| **Says what can't be fixed** | "If burned, start over as it cannot be fixed." He's honest about the point of no return. |
| **Gives credit freely** | "This is just a reformatting of Bertha's recipe." He links the source and tells you to buy the book. |
| **Invites checking** | "Trust this, or go down your own rabbit holes to see why it's true." He never says "trust me" alone. |
| **Marks opinion as opinion** | "In my opinion." "Seems to be optimum." |
| **Military shorthand, lightly** | "In-Brief", "Bottom line up front". Use it for summaries, not everywhere. |

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Explain *why* in a sentence, then move on | Lecture, or force-feed the reader |
| Name the incentive ("it's cheaper to make") | Name villains or hint at plots |
| Say "this worked for me" | Promise cures or outcomes |
| Keep it appealing and easy to try | Shame anyone for what they eat now |
| Use "toxic" and "poison" for specific, named things | Use fear words in headlines or as a hook |
| Let the reader decide ("use it or leave it") | Call for a revolution |

## Writing recipes

The recipe format exists for people cooking with limited energy, such as ME/CFS or long recovery. Write for that reader.

- **Lead with the verb in bold:** **Toast**, **Simmer**, **Strain**. Use one action per step where possible.
- **Say where it's safe to stop**, and say just as clearly when it isn't ("continue next steps immediately").
- **Give rests as rests:** "Cover and simmer at least 3 hours. Then go live your life."
- **Give sensory cues** (look, sound, smell) alongside times.
- **Rate every recipe in spoons**, and say what changes the rating (for example, "4 spoons if you do it without breaks").
- **Write a helper card for every recipe:** the steps and ratios that fit on one index card, for once you know it.
- Keep **From Experience** tips in Johnny's voice and in italics. That's where the personality goes.

## Health guardrails

- Johnny shares experience, not medical advice. The footer disclaimer stays on every page.
- When writing about healing, fasting or gut recovery, frame it as his experience. Keep his distinction of "fasting, not starving", and point people to their doctor, especially anyone chronically ill.
- When a claim is factual (a law, a date, a regulation), link the source.
